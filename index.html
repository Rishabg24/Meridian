The research practice of Dr. Rakesh Ghosh, PhD. Epidemiology of maternal, neonatal and environmental health.

[ What we do ]

Our Mission is to aid institutions, oranizations, community groups, and individuals in changing the world for the better. We conduct surveys, assess exposures, analyze data and create reports.

[ The consultant ]

Dr. Rakesh Ghosh, PhD

Epidemiologist · Principal Investigator

An epidemiologist specializing in maternal and neonatal health, environmental impacts, and quality improvement interventions, driven by a passion to advance science through rigorous research, impactful teaching, and purposeful collaboration.

The work integrates methodological rigor with advanced analytical expertise, producing a record of publications in high-impact journals, several of which have drawn international media coverage. An international background spanning India, the United Kingdom, and the United States has strengthened the ability to collaborate across cultures and disciplines, fostering inclusive learning environments and equitable research partnerships.

Two decades directing public health research across the United Kingdom, Europe, the United States, South Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Latin America. Principal Investigator on national and international studies.

  • Doctorate PhD, Epidemiology. Institute of Health and Society, Newcastle University, UK
  • Position Senior Epidemiologist, University of California, San Francisco. 2016–present
  • Committee National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. Scientific Review Committee
  • Fellowship Fellow of the Faculty of Public Health, Royal College of Physicians, UK
[ Reason for doing so ]

Numbers alone convince no one, and they save no one. Every dataset sits on top of a question someone was afraid to ask — who gets counted, who gets treated, who gets left outside the sampling frame. The work is not finished when the p-value is. It is finished when a ministry changes a policy, a clinic changes a protocol, or a community receives the resource the data said it needed.

[ What it changed ]

In 2019, reducing ambient and household PM2.5 exposure during pregnancy to a minimum risk level could have prevented almost 2.8 million low-birth-weight and 5.9 million preterm births across 204 countries and territories.

Ambient and household PM2.5 pollution and adverse perinatal outcomes. PLoS Medicine, 2021.
